# Research: Gear Systems in Other Games

How other games handle upgrading, enchanting and customising gear, and what that means for an enchanting system in Super Awesome Craft. For each game: the mechanic, what decisions it creates, whether it ends in one best-in-slot loadout, how it handles changing builds, and what it uses as a resource sink. Gathered November 2026 from official wikis (terraria.wiki.gg, minecraft.wiki, Factorio and Guild Wars 2 wikis, deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg), Blizzard and Grinding Gear Games posts, and guide sites where the wikis blocked automated reading (Stardew, Valheim, Zelda, Hollow Knight and Slay the Spire wikis all refused). Statements marked *(unverified)* come from one secondary source, from sources that disagree, or from general knowledge of the game that couldn't be checked against a page. Everything else is backed by the sources listed at the end.

## Minecraft Dungeons

**Mechanic.**

- Every weapon and armor piece has up to 3 enchantment slots. Each slot offers up to 3 random enchantments; you pick one and can level it to III.
- You spend **enchantment points**, earned one per hero level ("You'll earn an Enchantment Point each time you level up"). Costs climb per tier:
  - Common: 1, 2, 3 (6 total).
  - Powerful: 2, 3, 4 (9 total).
  - On gilded items: Common 2, 3, 4 and Powerful 3, 4, 5.
- **Refunds:** salvaging an item returns all points spent on it. The Blacksmith (which upgrades an item's power) also returns all points. The Enchantsmith returns the points from one enchantment.
- **Gilded items** come with an extra built-in enchantment at higher difficulties.
- **Power level:** each item has a power number; the hero's power comes from their gear and decides how hard a difficulty feels. Drops scale with difficulty, so better gear arrives by playing harder content *(unverified: the wiki page fetched didn't give the formula)*. The Blacksmith's result depends on the difficulty of the three missions played while it works.

**Decisions.** Which of three offers to take in each slot, how deep to go on each (one tier III or three tier Is), and which items deserve your limited points. Points are a single pool shared by the character, so investing in a weapon means not investing in armor *(unverified: the wiki doesn't state it outright, but players describe it this way)*.

**Best-in-slot.** The random three-per-slot offer means no two copies of an item are the same. The power number keeps climbing, though, so players constantly replace gear with a higher-power copy, which is a vertical treadmill the vision rejects.

**Respec.** Very cheap. Full refunds on salvage mean points are never lost, only parked. That makes experimenting painless.

**Sinks.** Points aren't consumed in the long run; the real sink is emeralds and the time spent farming higher power.

**Why it matters for us.** The clearest example of a budget that's spent across items, with a full refund, and a menu of three per slot. It's also learnable at a glance: the UI shows slots, choices and costs on the item itself.

## Terraria

**Modifiers (reforging).**

- Weapons, tools and functional accessories can carry one random modifier (also called a prefix), such as "Demonic Minishark". 83 exist. Armor and ammo can't have them.
- New items have up to a 75% chance of a modifier; shop items have none.
- The **Goblin Tinkerer** rerolls the modifier for one third of the item's buy value (20% cheaper with the Discount Card). Every eligible modifier has equal odds. Hitting the best one briefly locks the button and turns the name rainbow.
- Each class has a known best: Legendary for swung melee, Unreal for ranged, Mythical for magic. For accessories, the wiki calls Warding (+4 defense), Menacing (+4% damage) and Lucky (+4% crit) the consistently useful ones, with Warding "the best modifier in most scenarios".

**Accessories as horizontal choices.**

- 5 slots in Classic, 6 in Expert (with the Demon Heart), 7 in Master. **Duplicates can't be equipped.**
- Accessories combine at the **Tinkerer's Workshop** into one item with both effects (Hermes Boots and others up to the Terraspark Boots, for example). Combining frees slots, which is how the budget grows without the slot count growing.
- **Loadouts** (1.4.4): three sets of armor and accessories, switched with F1 to F3. Favoriting an accessory shares it across loadouts (1.4.5.7).

**World state drives the gear curve.**

- **Hardmode** starts permanently, for the whole world, the first time the Wall of Flesh dies. It reshapes the world (Hallow and evil stripes), adds NPCs and shop items, and gives the Pwnhammer that breaks altars.
- The three mechanical bosses drop **Hallowed Bars**, the only source. Killing all three makes Plantera available; killing Plantera opens further tiers. Each boss kill is a world flag.
- Gear tiers mostly follow ore tiers and boss drops; there's no per-item enchanting.

**Decisions.** Which accessories fit this fight (mobility, defense, damage, utility), which combination to craft, whether to keep rerolling.

**Best-in-slot.** Modifiers converge on one best per slot (so a "perfect" item exists), but accessory choice stays situational because no duplicates are allowed and many effects are utility rather than numbers.

**Respec.** Swapping is free; loadouts make it instant. Reforging can't be undone, but it can be redone.

**Sinks.** Reforging is the main coin sink. Chasing the best modifier usually costs more than the item is worth (see [durability research](durability.md#terraria-no-durability)).

## Monster Hunter: World and Rise

**Mechanic.**

- Gear is crafted **deterministically** from the parts of specific monsters: a Rathalos armor piece needs Rathalos scales, plates and so on. You know the recipe before the hunt, which makes "hunt this monster for this piece" the core loop.
- Each armor piece carries a few **skill points**. Points of the same skill add up across pieces, charms and decorations to a skill level, each skill with its own cap (Attack Boost caps at 7, for example).
- **Decoration slots** come in sizes 1 to 4 (Rise also has separate weapon-only Rampage slots). A decoration fits a slot of its size or bigger, and a small gem in a big slot wastes the space.
- **Set bonuses** activate when you wear enough pieces from one monster's set; Alpha, Beta and other variants of the same set count together.
- World's Alpha pieces have more built-in skills; Beta pieces have fewer skills and more slots. That's an explicit "fixed power or flexible slots" trade per piece *(unverified: PC Gamer guide summary)*.

**Where decorations come from.**

- **World:** random drops from high-level quests, graded C to S by rarity, plus a melding system. This was widely disliked. A Monster Hunter Wilds lead told press he "ended up finishing the game without having completed my build" because of World's decoration luck *(headline quote only; full interview not read)*.
- **Rise:** decorations are **crafted at the Smithy** from materials, unlocked by hunter rank and monster parts. Charms (talismans) went random instead. Players read this as Capcom moving the luck to a less central item.
- **Sunbreak's Qurious Crafting** added random augmentation to maxed armor. Social media filled with clips of augments *removing* skills, a reminder that randomness that can make gear worse feels bad *(press coverage)*.

**Decisions.** Which skill list your weapon and playstyle want, which pieces and slots reach it, and which monster to hunt next. Building a set is a puzzle with a budget (slots, skill caps, set-bonus thresholds) where the answer depends on the target monster.

**Best-in-slot.** There's a "meta" set per weapon at the top, but sets are tuned per weapon type and per monster (fire resistance, element weakness), so most players own many.

**Respec.** Gear never breaks, decorations can be moved between pieces at the equipment box *(unverified: widely known, not confirmed on a fetched page)*, and loadouts save whole builds. Owning several sets *is* the progression.

**Sinks.** Monster parts, consumed by each piece and each upgrade, plus rare parts that drop at low rates. Hunting the same monster again because you need four more plates is the intended repeat loop.

## Diablo 3 and Diablo 4

### Diablo 3: the Mystic and Kanai's Cube

- **Mystic enchanting** (Reaper of Souls):
  - You can reroll **one** property per item. Once you've changed one, the others lock for good.
  - The Mystic offers new options and the original, so you can always keep what you had.
  - Gold cost climbs with every reroll on the same item; legendaries also need a Forgotten Soul each time.
  - This is a choice (which property) with luck (what replaces it), and the lock stops infinite fine-tuning.
- **Kanai's Cube** (patch 2.3.0, 2015):
  - "Extract Legendary Power" **destroys an item and adds its legendary power to your catalogue of collected powers.** You can then equip that power without wearing the item.
  - You equip **one Weapon, one Armor and one Jewelry power** at a time. Season 22 added a fourth slot for any type, with no duplicates.
  - Extraction costs one each of five Act materials plus five Death's Breath (Maxroll).
  - The Cube also reforges legendaries completely and upgrades rares into legendaries.

**Why the Cube is the key pattern.** The library is **permanent and grows**; the **equipped slots are fixed**. Every new power you collect adds options without adding power, because you still only get three. Collecting is the long-term goal, choosing is the short-term one, and the item you destroyed stops cluttering your stash. Swapping powers costs nothing, so trying builds is free once the library has them *(unverified: free swapping is widely reported, not stated on a fetched page)*.

### Diablo 4: Codex of Power, Tempering and Masterworking

- **Codex of Power:**
  - Legendary powers ("aspects") live in a per-account library. Since Season 4 (May 2024), "Salvaging Legendary items stores their powers as Legendary Aspects in your Codex of Power to be reused indefinitely".
  - Salvaging a higher-rolled copy upgrades the stored aspect.
  - You **imprint** an aspect from the Codex onto an item at the Occultist, for gold and materials.
  - The Codex can be searched and filtered; a completed aspect gets a special border. That makes the library itself readable as a collection.
- **Tempering** (Season 4):
  - You learn **Tempering Manuals**, each with a small list of affixes. Once learned, a manual "can be used as many times as desired".
  - Applying one adds an affix of your choice from the manual, with a random value. Since Season 11 the randomness "only resides in the random value of the tempering affix".
  - Each item has **3 Tempering charges** (more for items with Greater Affixes). Rerolling spends a charge and can land worse. A rare Scroll of Restoration refills the charges.
- **Masterworking:** materials from an endgame dungeon raise all affixes, and every fourth rank greatly upgrades one affix, chosen at random. Failure chances were removed in Season 4. You can reset and start over.
- **Occultist enchanting** rerolls one affix; in Season 4 its gold cost got a maximum.

**Decisions.** Which aspect goes on which slot, which manual affix to temper, whether to gamble a charge.

**Best-in-slot.** Every build has a known best set of aspects, but there are many builds per class and seasons reshuffle them. The Masterworking random target is the "near-endless" ceiling.

**Respec.** The library makes respec mostly a matter of re-imprinting. Diablo's lesson: keeping the *knowledge* permanent (the Codex, learned manuals) while keeping *applications* costly (gold, materials, charges) makes experimenting possible without making it free.

**Sinks.** Destroyed items, gold (rerolls, imprints) and endgame materials.

## Last Epoch and Path of Exile (brief)

**Last Epoch.**

- Every item has a pool of **Forging Potential**, larger for rarer items from higher-level areas. You add or upgrade an affix of your choice using a matching **affix shard**, and each craft costs a random amount of potential (Maxroll's example: a tier 3 to 4 upgrade costs 1 to 18). At 0 the item can't be crafted any further.
- The **Glyph of Hope** gives a 25% chance that a craft costs nothing. The **Rune of Removal** removes a random affix and refunds it as shards. **Shattering** destroys an item for shards.
- This is "choose the outcome, gamble on how many tries you get". Players like it far more than pure rerolls, because every step is chosen. The randomness sits on the budget rather than on the result.
- Forging Potential is close to our Capacity idea, except it's spent by *attempts* rather than held by *what's installed*.

**Path of Exile.**

- There's no gold. "Currency" items are crafting consumables: the Chaos Orb rerolls a rare, and so on. Grinding Gear Games' 2013 reasoning: "Games that use gold incur high inflation unless they have sufficient gold sinks", and "By having every currency item inherently useful to improve a character, each currency item is its own sink", which let them drop "unnecessary gold sinks like repairing items".
- **Pitfalls:**
  - The most common orbs became trade money first and crafting tools second. Most players buy items rather than craft, because rerolling an item hundreds of times is worse than trading *(unverified: commentary, not a GGG statement)*.
  - Deterministic crafting seasons (Harvest, 2020) made top players dramatically richer and had to be pulled back *(unverified: secondary analysis)*.
  - The whole system is unreadable without outside tools and guides.
- For us, the useful part is the **sink logic** (crafting materials that are also what you'd trade), and the warning: once a crafting material is the most liquid good, it becomes the universal currency the vision blames for the last playtest.

## Valheim

**Mechanic.**

- Each **biome** gives the materials for the next tier: copper and tin in the Black Forest, iron in the Swamp, silver in the Mountains, black metal in the Plains, and onward. Gear is crafted at a **Workbench** or **Forge**.
- Stations level up when you build **extensions** next to them (chopping block, tanning rack, adze, tool shelf on the Workbench), from level 1 to 5.
- Each item can be upgraded to higher **quality** levels with more of its materials. Each quality level needs a higher station level, roughly one station level per quality step *(unverified: secondary guide)*. Higher quality raises armor, damage and durability.
- **Boss kills set world-wide "global keys"** (for example `defeated_eikthyr`). These change raid events and what the world does for everyone, not just the killer *(community wiki; Iron Gate doesn't document it)*. Recipes, on the other hand, are learned per player when they first pick up a material *(unverified)*.

**Decisions.** Few. Within a biome tier there are a couple of armor sets and weapon types, but the newest biome's set usually outclasses older ones. The real decision is which station extensions to build first.

**Best-in-slot.** Effectively yes per tier. Valheim relies on each new biome to reset the target.

**Respec.** Build another item; materials are the only cost. Repair is free at a station (see [durability research](durability.md#valheim-durability-with-free-repair)).

**Sinks.** Biome materials, in large amounts for quality upgrades and base building. The station-extension pattern is notable: the base itself becomes the gating mechanism, and it's shared by everyone who uses that base.

## Stardew Valley

**Mechanic (the Forge, in the Volcano Dungeon).**

- **Gem forging:** a melee weapon can be forged up to 3 times, each with a gem (Ruby for damage, Aquamarine for crit chance and so on) and 10, 15, then 20 Cinder Shards. A Diamond fills all remaining forges at random.
- **Enchanting:** one Prismatic Shard and 20 Cinder Shards give a tool or melee weapon a **random** enchantment. Doing it again replaces the current one.
- **Innate enchantments** (1.6): a Dragon Tooth and 10 Cinder Shards roll random innate stats on a melee weapon *(secondary source)*.
- Rings can be combined in the Forge; the Galaxy Soul turns a Galaxy weapon into an Infinity weapon.

**Decisions.** Gem forging is a real choice (damage, speed, crit). Enchanting is a slot machine: you know the list, not the result.

**Best-in-slot.** Converges quickly: the community knows the best enchant per tool, and Prismatic Shards are rare enough that rolling for it feels bad.

**Respec.** Re-enchanting replaces, so there's no lock-in, just more shards.

**Sinks.** Prismatic Shards (rare and wanted for other things) and Cinder Shards. Stardew is the negative example of putting randomness on the one expensive step.

## Deep Rock Galactic

**Mechanic.**

- Each primary and secondary weapon has **five tiers of modifications** with two to three upgrades per tier, and "only one modification can be equipped per tier". Tiers unlock with class level (primaries at 0, 4, 8, 12 and 16).
- Each mod is bought once with Credits plus specific cave minerals (a Tier 1 mod 1,200 Credits and 25 Croppa; a Tier 5 mod 5,600 Credits plus three minerals).
- Once bought, you can **switch between mods in a tier freely** at the terminal, and loadouts save setups *(unverified: well known, not stated on fetched pages)*.
- **Overclocks** unlock after your first promotion:
  - They come from **Overclock-infused Matrix Cores**, which drop from weekly Deep Dives, Core Hunt assignments, events and the Core Infuser machine event.
  - The specific overclock is decided at random when you get the core, then crafted at the Forge with Credits and minerals and kept for good.
  - Types: **Clean** (a boost with no penalty), **Balanced** (a bigger boost with an equal penalty), **Unstable** (the biggest boost, heavy penalties, often a new mechanic).
  - You need at least one mod in every tier before you can equip one, and you equip one per weapon *(unverified)*.

**Decisions.** Every tier is a sideways pick (damage vs ammo vs fire rate, or a mechanic), and the overclock decides what the weapon *is*. Unstable overclocks are pure trade-offs.

**Best-in-slot.** Players argue over best builds, but the tier structure (one of three, no stacking) and many team roles keep several builds alive per weapon.

**Respec.** Free after purchase. The cost is paid once to *learn* an option, not to *use* it.

**Sinks.** Credits and minerals per mod and overclock, which gives cave minerals a reason to exist. Found cores are a **loot-driven collection**: you can't grind a specific overclock directly, which keeps finding them exciting.

## Choice architecture: Hades and Slay the Spire (brief)

These are roguelikes, so builds reset each run, but they're the cleanest examples of how to present a choice.

- **Hades:** each god offers **one boon from three**. Boons come in rarities, can be levelled (Pom of Power), and **Duo boons** only appear once you hold boons from both gods, which rewards committing. The permanent **Mirror of Night** upgrades can be refunded at no cost, and each one has a paired alternative you can swap to *(unverified: general knowledge of the game)*. The pattern: permanent unlocks plus free switching between paired alternatives.
- **Slay the Spire:** each card reward is "choose one of three cards, or skip". Skipping matters because a lean deck is stronger, so "no" is a real decision. The Singing Bowl relic turns a skip into +2 max HP. Mega Crit settled on about 75 cards per character as the right pool size *(wiki summary)*.
- **What carries over:** offers of three are easy to read; a skip option makes every offer a decision; prerequisites (Duo boons) reward commitment without forcing it.

## Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom (brief)

- **Great Fairies** upgrade armor for monster parts and rupees. You can only upgrade as many levels as fairies you've found (four), so the upgrade path is unlocked by **exploring**, not by grinding.
- Many sets have a **set bonus** that only works when the full set is worn and upgraded *(secondary: the level needed varies by source)*.
- Sets are situational (cold, heat, stealth, climbing, swimming), so you carry several and swap. Upgrades consume large amounts of monster parts, which gives fighting a lasting purpose.
- The pattern: **upgrade capacity unlocked by a world discovery**, and materials spent per level on whichever set you care about.

## Other budget examples

### Hollow Knight: charm notches

- You start with 3 **notches** and can reach 11. Each charm costs 1 to 5. Notches come from exploration, a shop that unlocks as you collect charms, and late challenges.
- Charms can only be changed while sitting at a **bench**.
- **Overcharming:** you can equip past your limit, but you take double damage. That's a deliberate "break the budget, at a price" valve.
- The nearest thing to our Capacity idea in a well-loved game: the budget grows with exploration, the costs are printed on each charm, and the rule fits on one line.

### Destiny 2: armor energy

- Each armor piece has **energy** up to 10; each mod costs a fixed amount (a helmet mod might cost 3). Upgrading a piece's energy costs materials (Bungie help article; guide sites).
- In January 2023, before Lightfall, Bungie **unlocked every standard armor mod for everyone**, so the collection stopped being a grind and the build choice became the point.
- The pattern: capacity *per item* that you raise with materials, plus a *shared, permanent* list of what you can put in it.

## Shared progression of crafting knowledge

| Game | What's shared | How it's paid for |
|---|---|---|
| **Factorio** | "Research is shared among all members of a force." Each force has its own technology tree | Science packs consumed by labs; infinite research at the top (since 0.15) |
| **Satisfactory** | Milestones unlock tiers for the session *(unverified that it's shared, but it's one HUB per world)* | Parts delivered to the HUB Terminal, which "cannot be taken back"; Space Elevator phases gate tiers |
| **Guild Wars 2** | Guild hall upgrades are guild-wide and permanent | Any member donates materials to the guild treasury; plus Favor from weekly missions and mined Aetherium |
| **Terraria** | Hardmode and every boss kill are world flags; NPC shops follow | Beating the boss |
| **Valheim** | Global keys after each boss change the world for everyone | Beating the boss. Recipes stay per player *(unverified)* |
| **Diablo 4** | The Codex of Power is shared by every character on the account | Salvaging legendaries |

The common thread: **knowledge is shared, items are personal.** Factorio and Satisfactory make research a world project everyone feeds; Guild Wars 2 makes donation the way any member, of any playstyle, contributes. Satisfactory's permanent deliveries and GW2's treasury are both sinks that turn surplus materials into shared progress.

## Patterns across these games

### What prevents one best-in-slot endpoint

- **Fixed slots with sideways options** (DRG tiers, Kanai's Cube slots, Hollow Knight notches). Power doesn't stack, so new options add variety, not height.
- **No duplicates** (Terraria accessories, the Cube's extra slot).
- **Situational value** (Monster Hunter elements, Zelda environments, Terraria utility accessories). The best set depends on the target.
- **Trade-offs printed on the option** (DRG Balanced and Unstable overclocks, Hollow Knight overcharming, Alpha vs Beta).
- **What fails:** a power number that keeps rising (Dungeons, Diablo item power) or a newest-tier set that obsoletes everything (Valheim) makes a treadmill even when the build choices are horizontal.

### How games handle changing builds

- **Pay to learn, not to use** (DRG mods, Diablo 4 manuals, the Codex, Destiny 2 mods). Once you own an option, switching is free or cheap. This is the most player-friendly model, and it pairs naturally with no durability.
- **Full refunds** (Dungeons salvage). Builds are never wasted.
- **Locks** (D3's one-property rule, Last Epoch's potential) stop endless fine-tuning, at the cost of some "my item is ruined" frustration.

### Where randomness helps and hurts

- **Helps:** on *finding* options (DRG cores, D3 legendary drops, Dungeons' three offers). It makes loot exciting, and choice still decides the build.
- **Hurts:** on the *final expensive step* (Stardew's Prismatic enchant, World's decorations, Qurious Crafting removing skills). Capcom moving decorations to crafting in Rise is the clearest developer correction found.
- **Middle ground:** choose the outcome, randomise the cost or value (Last Epoch, D4 Tempering since Season 11).

### Sinks without durability

Every game here without durability (Terraria, Monster Hunter, DRG, Dungeons) sinks materials into **making more things** (more sets, more mods, more slots), not into maintaining one thing. Path of Exile's argument is the same: when the crafting material is its own sink, repair isn't needed.

## Fit with SuperAwesomeCraft

Our constraints: no XP, no durability, a Capacity budget per item, Catalyst recipes, a library of Chiseled Bookshelves, shared world progression on a 5 to 10 player server, "Convenience is cheap, Power is earned", and everything learnable in-game.

### Patterns worth borrowing

1. **The extract-to-library pattern (Kanai's Cube, the Codex).** Our library of Chiseled Bookshelves already resembles this. Push it further:
   - Putting an enchanted book (or "extracting" an enchanted item) into the library **records** that enchantment for good. The table can then make it from Catalysts.
   - The library is **per Settlement, so shared by everyone who uses it**, which makes it the server's shared crafting knowledge, the way Factorio research is shared by a force.
   - The collection is the open-ended goal (a Collection Log for enchantments); the item's Capacity is the fixed budget. New finds add options, not height, which serves "no single max-gear endpoint".
2. **Capacity as a printed budget (Hollow Knight, Destiny 2, Dungeons).** Show each item's Capacity and each enchantment's cost right in the tooltip and the table UI. Raising Capacity comes from Era materials (Destiny's energy upgrades, Zelda's fairies) and never from use.
3. **Pay to learn, cheap to swap (DRG, Destiny 2, D4 manuals).** With no durability, gear is permanent, so let players **remove an enchantment from an item with a full or near-full refund** (Dungeons' salvage). Building a second specialised tool should feel like the progression, not a punishment for changing your mind.
4. **Offers of three, with a skip (Dungeons, Hades, Slay the Spire)**, but only where luck belongs: loot books, fishing treasure, Stronghold rewards. The table itself stays deterministic, which matches the Ascendant Arcana leaning and Rise's correction.
5. **Sideways choices printed with trade-offs (DRG overclocks, Alpha vs Beta).** Rarer, Stronghold-found enchantments could be "Balanced" or "Unstable": strong, with a stated cost. That keeps late Power exciting without simply being bigger.
6. **No duplicates, situational value (Terraria, Monster Hunter, Zelda).** Pairs well with the *parallel materials* Idea in the design map: several sets per Era, each good at something.
7. **Upgrades unlocked by world discovery (Zelda's fairies, Terraria's Hardmode, Valheim's stations).** The next tier of enchanting (more Capacity, rarer Catalysts) opens when a Stronghold falls, for the whole world. Valheim's station extensions suggest the table's power could come from blocks placed around it, which our library already does.
8. **Shared donation as the sink (Guild Wars 2 treasury, Satisfactory's HUB).** A Settlement-level project that consumes surplus iron, diamonds and Era materials to raise the library's power or unlock Catalyst recipes for everyone. Any playstyle can contribute materials, so this serves "every playstyle pushes the world forward" and replaces durability as a sink.
9. **Convenience vs Power split in the menu itself.** Following DRG's free swaps, Convenience enchantments (Silk Touch, Efficiency) could be cheap in Capacity and Catalysts, available early from a basic library; Power enchantments cost more Capacity and need Era Catalysts.

### Patterns to avoid

- **A rising power number** (Dungeons, Diablo item power). It rebuilds the private ladder the vision blames.
- **Randomness on the expensive final step** (Stardew, World's decorations, Qurious Crafting). It's the opposite of learnable and makes slow players fall further behind.
- **A crafting material that is also the best trade good** (Path of Exile's orbs). If one Catalyst becomes what everyone wants, it becomes the new emerald.
- **Locks that ruin items** (D3's one-property lock, Last Epoch at 0 potential). Without durability, gear is meant to be kept; a mechanic that "bricks" it fights that.
- **Systems readable only through a wiki** (Path of Exile, Monster Hunter's skill math). Our tooltips and table screen should show the whole decision.

### Learnability checklist (from what the readable systems share)

- The budget is one number on the item, with costs next to each option (Hollow Knight, Destiny 2, Dungeons).
- The menu shows locked options and what unlocks them (DRG's tier levels, D4's Codex borders).
- Changing your mind is cheap, so learning by trying is safe (DRG, Dungeons).
- The rule fits in one sentence: "each enchantment costs Capacity; each item holds so much."

### Questions for Season 1

- Is the library per player, per Settlement, or world-wide? Shared fits the vision; per player fits "collecting" better. A mix (the world records it, a Settlement's shelves enable it) is possible.
- What does removing an enchantment refund: the Catalysts, the book, or both?
- Do Convenience enchantments cost Capacity at all, or do they sit outside it so building never competes with Power?
- Where does randomness live: book loot, fishing and Stronghold offers, but never the table?
- What sinks surplus iron and diamonds once nothing wears out: Capacity upgrades, library projects, or both?
