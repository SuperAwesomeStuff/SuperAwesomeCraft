Enchanting: three options

Season 1 replaces vanilla enchanting. Here are three complete designs. Read them, argue about them, and tell us what you like and what you don't. We'll either pick one or mix the best parts and go round again.

Overview Same in all three A · Capacity B · The Archive C · Runes Side by side Your feedback

The short version

Last season, people hit full Netherite (or killed the Dragon) and then had nothing left to want. All three designs try to fix that by giving you something to chase after full Netherite, without making the early game a grind.

Option A

Catalysts and Capacity

Every weapon and armour piece has a points budget. Strong enchantments cost more points, so you can't have everything on one item. Books found in dungeons unlock the higher levels for everyone.

Feels like: building a loadout on a points budget.

Option B

The Archive

The whole server researches enchantments together by donating materials. Everything researched belongs to everyone. You pick which enchantments go in your item's few slots, and do a personal trip to push your favourite items to max.

Feels like: a server-wide tech tree everyone chips into.

Option C

Runes

Enchantments are physical runes you carve, keep and pop into sockets on your gear. Swapping is free and nothing is ever lost. You can give spare runes to friends.

Feels like: collecting and slotting gems, like Monster Hunter or Hollow Knight charms.

What's the same in all three

These rules apply whichever option we pick, so you don't need to compare them.

The big changes

  • No XP. Nothing costs levels. (Already built.)
  • No durability. Your gear never breaks, so a build is something you keep.
  • Mending and Unbreaking are gone, since nothing breaks.
  • The Enchanting Table does everything. Same block, new screen. Every recipe and cost is shown before you pay, so you never need a wiki.
  • The anvil only renames (free). No more "too expensive".
  • Tools have their own system (Familiarity and Techniques: vein-mining, tree-felling and so on), separate from enchanting.

Comfort is cheap, power is earned

Enchantments are split into three groups:

  • Convenience (Efficiency, Silk Touch, Feather Falling, Depth Strider, Respiration, Aqua Affinity, Soul Speed, Swift Sneak, Frost Walker, Lure, Loyalty, Riptide): cheap, available on day one, no fighting needed, and they never use up your budget.
  • Yield (Fortune, Looting, Luck of the Sea): the top level needs a far-off material, because extra loot snowballs.
  • Power (Sharpness, Protection, Power and the rest): uses your item's budget. This is where the choices are.

Loot, fishing and villagers

  • Anything random gives at most level II of a Power or Yield enchantment: fishing, chest loot, mob gear and villager trades. An AFK fish farm still works, but it won't hand out Looting III.
  • Villagers never sell Power or Yield books. Librarians sell comfort enchantments only.
  • Every player gets their own chest loot (an existing mod like Lootr). Nobody can loot the Ancient City first and leave the rest of you with empty chests.
  • Master Cartographers sell an Ancient City map.
  • Loot you find with enchantments already on it keeps working as found.

Option A

Catalysts and Capacity

Pay a fixed recipe for each enchantment level and fit it into your item's Capacity. Books found in the world unlock the higher levels for everyone, and far-off trips raise your Capacity.

What playing it looks like

  1. First eveningYou craft an Enchanting Table. The screen lists everything your pickaxe can take, each with its recipe. You put Efficiency II and Silk Touch on it for some Redstone and Amethyst. Tools have no budget, so there's nothing to think about.
  2. First weekYour diamond sword shows Capacity 0/10. Sharpness V costs 5 and Looting III costs 9, so you can't have both. You make one Looting sword for the mob farm and one Sharpness + Fire Aspect sword for fighting. Sharpness III is greyed out until someone finds a Sharpness book in a dungeon. A friend finds one, registers it at a table, and chat says "[friend] added Sharpness to the library (9/22)". Now everyone can make Sharpness III and IV.
  3. After full NetheriteLooting III needs Echo Shards, so you buy an Ancient City map and go. You bring back extra shards and Attune your chestplate: its Capacity goes from 8 to 10, so it now fits Protection IV and Steadfast II, and you're silent to Sculk while wearing it.

How it works

1. Capacity: your item's points budget

Weapons and armour have a Capacity. Only Power and Yield enchantments spend it; comfort enchantments are free. Pickaxes, shovels, hoes and fishing rods have no Capacity at all. Axes count as weapons ("anything you hit things with has Capacity").

2. Recipes: pick, pay, done

Each level has a fixed recipe shown on screen. No randomness. Higher levels use materials from further up the ladder. Buying level III when you have nothing pays for I, II and III in one click. Some examples:

3. The library: found books unlock levels for everyone

Levels I and II are open from the start. Level III and up of an enchantment unlock for the whole server once someone registers a book of it that they found in a structure chest (dungeons, mineshafts, shipwrecks, strongholds, Bastions, Ancient Cities). The top level also needs its expensive recipe.

  • Registering is one click on the table's Library tab. You get the book straight back, and chat announces it with your name.
  • Unlocks are instant and permanent, and they count at every table in the world.
  • Fishers and mob farmers help too: 3 ordinary books of an enchantment from 2 different players count as one found book.
  • A found book from somewhere far-off (Ancient City, End City or ominous vault) also halves that enchantment's top-level recipe for everyone, so late-game trips still help the server.

4. Changing your build: Extract

Pull one enchantment off an item onto a plain book, for free, and put it on something else or give it to a friend. The catch: top levels (Sharpness V, Looting III, Protection IV and so on) come off one level lower, so you pay the top step again on the new item. The screen warns you before you confirm. Smithing diamond to netherite keeps everything, so you never need to extract for that.

This is A's identity: the top of every build is consumed. Lower levels move freely and can be handed down to newer players.

5. Attunement: the long goal

Each weapon or armour piece can be Attuned up to three times, with a different far-off material each time. Each Attunement gives +2 Capacity and that material's bonus effect. A fully Attuned netherite sword (18) fits Sharpness V, Looting III and Fire Aspect II, but still not everything. Tools can be Attuned for the effect only.

Other details

  • The grindstone refuses items with Power or Yield enchantments, so you can't misclick away something you paid for (use Extract instead).
  • Wind Burst can't be made until someone registers a Wind Burst book from an ominous vault.

What's good about it

  • The closest to enchanting we've had before (Super Awesome Enchanting, Ascendant Arcana), with the bad parts removed.
  • Easy first hour: tools have no budget, and every lock tells you exactly what unlocks it.
  • Real build choices: you'll want several specialised items, not one god sword.
  • Explorers matter: finding a book in a dungeon unlocks levels for everyone, with your name in chat.
  • The simplest of the three to build.

The catches

  • Moving a top level to another item costs the top step again.
  • Lots of recipes (about 130), though the screen shows each one.
  • The library probably fills up in the first week or two, so the shared part goes quiet later on.
  • A fast player could finish their items and run out of goals.
  • Some arithmetic: you're adding up points.
Option B

The Archive

Every enchantment is known at level I from day one. The server researches higher levels together by filling material bills, and everything researched belongs to everyone. You pick what goes in your item's few slots, and Masterwork your favourite items for the final level.

What playing it looks like

  1. First eveningEvery Enchanting Table opens the Archive, a book of every enchantment. Comfort enchantments are known at every level, so Efficiency and Silk Touch go on your pick for free. Everything else is known at level I, so you slot Looting I on your sword.
  2. First weekThe Research page shows bills. Fire Aspect II needs Blaze Rods, Magma Cream and Coal. You drop in spare Coal, someone else adds the Blaze Rods, and when the bar fills, everyone's Fire Aspect becomes Fire Aspect II. The World Sharpness bill slowly fills with iron and gold that would otherwise sit in chests.
  3. After full NetheriteYour sword has its slots and World Sharpness is at III. You Masterwork it with Echo Shards: everything on it goes up a step (Sharpness IV, Looting III), you're silent to Sculk while holding it, and it carries your name. Later, a Heart of the Sea Masterwork lets you swim faster too.

How it works

1. Research: shared bills

Every level above I has a bill: a list of materials anyone can donate to, at any table. When a bill fills, that level is known for the whole server, everyone's slotted enchantments upgrade automatically, and chat names every contributor.

  • Research takes each enchantment to one level below its max (Looting II, Smite IV, Fire Protection III). The last level is personal, through Masterwork. Two-level enchantments (Fire Aspect, Knockback, Punch, Venom, Frostbite) research all the way.
  • Every bill needs at least three different materials from at least two tiers, so no single farm can fill one.
  • The page features three bills at a time so effort isn't spread thin, but anyone can donate to any bill. It also shows which bills take which materials, so a player holding Rotten Flesh can see where it helps.
  • Enchanted books you find can pay a little toward their own enchantment's bill, so loot is never junk.
  • The whole Archive is about 49 bills, sized to roughly the playtime the whole group put in last season.

3. Slots: your personal choice

Each slot holds one enchantment at the highest level the server knows (or lower, if you prefer). Swapping is free, any time, at any table. Comfort enchantments don't use slots.

ItemSlots
Swords, axes, spears1 (up to iron) · 2 (diamond) · 3 (netherite)
Bow, mace2
Crossbow, trident1
Armour1 (netherite: 2): Thorns, Steadfast, or a specialised Protection
Pickaxe, shovel, hoe, fishing rod1: Fortune or Luck of the Sea

Netherite gets an extra slot, so upgrading to netherite is a personal step forward before any Masterwork. Example diamond sword loadouts: Looting II + Fire Aspect II for the mob farm; Smite IV + Knockback II for a skeleton spawner; Venom II + Sweeping Edge II for a raid.

4. Masterwork: your personal final step

Each item can be Masterworked up to twice, with a different far-off material each time. Each gives that material's bonus effect. The first one also takes everything on the item, world stats included, up its final level: World Sharpness V, Smite V, Looting III, Protection IV. This is the only way to reach max levels, so the top of power is always earned by your own trip. The item gets stamped with your name.

  • The final step applies to whatever was slotted when you Masterworked. Moving it to different enchantments later costs a small tier 2 material.
  • Pickaxes can be Masterworked too: that's how you get Fortune III, and a Heart of the Sea works, so you never need to fight for it.
B reads "power is earned" at the group level: someone who never fights still gets World Sharpness IV, because the group earned it. Only the final level is personal. If that feels wrong in play, a prepared fix is that world stats only apply to players who've contributed to bills.

What's good about it

  • The most "we built this together" option. Every stack of Bones counts, and you're named in the credits.
  • Best for slower players and late joiners: you get everything the server knows the moment you arrive.
  • A fast player can't out-gear everyone; what they fund helps the whole group.
  • Easiest first hour: everything works at level I, swaps are free, and nothing can be lost.
  • Gives a real use for the stacks of iron and gold sitting in chests.

The catches

  • Less personal: most of your power comes from the group, and gear-focused players might feel their effort is diluted.
  • Free swapping makes slots feel more like a loadout than a build.
  • Armour has very few choices (one slot on most armour).
  • The second Masterwork only adds a bonus effect, not power.
  • Once the Archive is finished, there's not much left to donate to.
  • The biggest of the three to build.
Option C

Runes

Every Power and Yield enchantment is a rune: an item you carve, keep, and slot into your gear's sockets. Runes come out intact whenever you like, so your collection is your progress. The top rank of each rune is Grand and needs a socket Gilded with a far-off material.

Four new words: rune socket Gilded Grand. Everything else is vanilla.

What playing it looks like

  1. First eveningYou put your pick in the Enchanting Table and click Efficiency II and Silk Touch for a few Redstone and Amethyst. Comfort enchantments go straight on and don't use a socket. Then you carve your first rune, Looting I, from Bones, Gold, Lapis and Amethyst.
  2. First weekYour diamond sword has 3 sockets. You slot Sharpness IV, Looting II and Knockback I. For the Wither you swap Sharpness IV for Smite IV from your Rune Pouch. Nothing is lost; the Sharpness rune goes back in the pouch. You hand a spare Looting II to a friend for their farm sword, with your name on it.
  3. After full NetheriteSharpness V is a Grand rune, and its recipe says "needs a Gilded socket" before you carve it. You Gild a socket with a Heart of the Sea (which also lets you swim faster), then carve the Grand rune. Your old spare Sharpness IV goes to a friend who just reached diamond.

How it works

1. Runes

A rune is one enchantment at one rank, like Looting Rune II. Its tooltip says what it does, what it fits on and who carved it. Each enchantment family has its own colour, and Grand runes get a gold frame.

  • Carving rank I: the enchantment's material plus 2 Lapis and 1 Amethyst.
  • Ranking up: a rune of the rank below plus the next material. The lower rune gets used up, so having Sharpness IV on two items means carving it twice.
  • Grand runes are the top rank of every enchantment with three or more levels (Sharpness V, Protection IV, Looting III, Fortune III, Thorns III and so on). They need a far-off or Nether material and only fit in a Gilded socket.

3. Gilding: the long goal

Gild one socket with one of the five far-off materials. Each material can Gild only one socket per item, and each gives its bonus effect. Grand runes need a Gilded socket. Nobody has to Gild: Protection III, Sharpness IV and Looting II fit ordinary sockets, so solid gear never needs a far-off trip. A pickaxe's single socket counts as already Gilded, so Fortune III only costs its own recipe.

4. Sharing: first carvings and gifts

  • First carvings: the first time anyone carves a middle rank (say, the world's first Sharpness IV), that rank's materials are halved for everyone, and chat names the carver. The table shows a server-wide count (First carvings 12/46). The world's first Grand runes get a chat shout-out too.
  • Gifting: any rune can be given away, Grand ones included, with the carver's name on it. The receiver still needs their own Gilded socket for a Grand rune, so a gift doesn't skip the trip.

5. The Rune Pouch

A bundle that holds up to 64 runes (Leather and String). It shows how many of the 31 enchantments you have a rune for. A dropped pouch can't burn, be blown up or despawn, so your whole collection isn't lost to one lava pool. Runes socketed in gear still drop with that gear when you die, as normal.

Other details

  • Putting an enchanted book in the table breaks it into runes. Found loot gear's enchantments count as runes and can be pulled out.
  • The grindstone refuses items with runes, so you can't misclick your collection away.
  • Comfort enchantments are cheap and never use a socket: "Comfort uses no socket; power is a rune."

What's good about it

  • The most fun to tinker with: carving, collecting and slotting physical runes.
  • Nothing is ever lost when you change your mind. Experimenting is free.
  • Your collection is visible progress, and the pouch counter is a goal in itself.
  • Generous players shine: spare runes equip friends straight away, with your name on them.
  • The pouch protects you from the worst "I lost everything" moment.

The catches

  • The most new things to learn (four new words, ranking up, Gilding, the pouch).
  • Once everyone has their runes, carving could slow down (the "collection plateau").
  • The shared part (first carvings) will mostly happen early and probably go to whoever plays most.
  • A Grand rune lost in lava takes its Gilding with it.
  • Lots of new art and UI to build (runes, pouch, sockets).

You'll probably like…

A

…if you enjoy planning builds on a budget, owning several specialised items, and you like the idea that finding a dungeon book helps the whole server.

B

…if you want enchanting to feel like a group project, you play less than others and don't want to fall behind, or you like donating to a shared goal.

C

…if you love collecting things, swapping loadouts for different fights, and giving stuff to friends.

Mixing is allowed. These are packages, but parts can move between them. For example: C's runes with B's shared research, or A's Capacity with C's free swapping. If you love one piece of an option and hate the rest, say so.
