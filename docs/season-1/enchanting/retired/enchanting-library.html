Enchanting: The Library

Enchanting starts early, and you build a library of enchanted books that unlocks stronger enchantments and makes enchanting cheap. This replaces the three options from last time.

Why How it works The library Costs Alone or together Good and bad Your feedback

What enchanting is for

Enchanting is how your gear grows through the whole game. It starts cheap with iron-age stuff, climbs as you reach further places, and the library you build shows how far you've come.
Growth

A long climb, not a late burst

Instead of nothing until diamonds and then everything at once, your gear gets better bit by bit from the first evening to the far corners of the world.

Something to spend on

Ore still matters

No durability and no XP means materials need a use. Making books for your library eats a lot of them.

Something to build

A real place

Your library is a room you build, fill and show off. It's your progress, sitting on shelves.

What changed since last time

  • It starts early. In vanilla, you need a diamond pickaxe for obsidian before you can even think about enchanting. Now the table is a book, 2 lapis and 4 amethyst blocks.
  • No invisible unlocks. Nothing switches on for the whole server behind the scenes. Everything is real blocks and books you can see.
  • Books live on shelves. A book only counts on a Chiseled Bookshelf near the table, and the table has a chest-style tab for shelving them without walking away.
  • No item limits. No "capacity" budget on items, and no tool levelling. Just enchanting.
  • No new enchantments. Just vanilla's, minus Mending and Unbreaking (nothing breaks any more). New ones might come later.

How it works

A quick story of one enchantment, from start to finish.

  1. Day one: build a tableCraft an enchanting table from a book, 2 lapis and 4 amethyst blocks. On its own, it can write a level I book of any enchantment, if you pay the materials.
  2. Write Efficiency IPay 16 copper and 8 lapis. You get an Efficiency I book.
  3. Shelve itOpen the table's Library tab and drop the book into an empty shelf slot, just like putting something in a chest. Two things happen: the table now offers the Efficiency II book recipe, and putting Efficiency I on your gear becomes cheap (2 copper, 1 lapis), and the book is never used up.
  4. Keep climbingWrite Efficiency II, shelve it, and so on up to V. Efficiency only needs early materials, so a builder can get there without fighting anything.
  5. Power takes travelCombat and loot enchantments climb further. Fortune III's book needs Echo Shards from an Ancient City. Sharpness V needs Shulker Shells from End Cities. The very top levels also cost one of those rare materials every time you put them on an item.

Books never go straight onto gear. You can't slap a book onto your sword any more. Every enchantment is done at the table, from a book on a shelf.

Taking an enchantment off an item is free (you don't get anything back). If your library has the book, putting it back is cheap, so trying things out is safe.

The library

Your library is every Chiseled Bookshelf near the table, up to 6 blocks away sideways and 3 up or down. Walls and decorations in between don't matter, so you can build a proper room. Books only count on a shelf; one in your pocket does nothing.

One Chiseled Bookshelf holds six books. This one lets you enchant everything up to those levels cheaply, and write Sharpness IV and Fortune III next. Efficiency V, Silk Touch and Protection IV are already the top level.

  • Only enchanted books count. Normal bookshelves are just decoration.
  • Book spines are coloured by type, and top-level books get a gold mark, so you can read a library from across the room.
  • A book counts for its own level and every level below it.
  • Several tables can share one library.
  • A library is just blocks. Nobody owns it, and anyone can take a book out. Take a book out and the library loses what it unlocked until the book goes back. Enchantments already on your gear never go away. Someone will take one eventually, so keep spare copies; they make writing cheaper anyway.

The Library tab

The table has a tab that works like a big chest made of every shelf around it. Click, shift-click and drag books between it and your inventory like you always do. Empty slots show how much room is left, and a Sort button tidies everything by type, enchantment and level (only if you press it, so hand-arranged shelves stay as they are).

Chipping in

Expensive books don't have to be paid by one person. A table can hold a half-paid book, and anyone can drop materials in whenever they pass by. It finishes the moment the recipe is full. What goes in can't be taken back out, and breaking the table drops it all, like a chest.

Spares

Every other book of the same enchantment on your shelves (a duplicate, or a lower level) takes 20% off writing the next level, up to 60%. It never touches the rare far-away material, and spares aren't used up. So keeping Efficiency I to IV on the shelf while you write V is worth it, and duplicate books from chests and fishing always have a use.

Found books

  • Chests, fishing and villagers still give enchanted books. Combat and loot enchantments from these are capped at level II, so a fish farm can't skip the climb.
  • Every found book is useful once it's shelved: it unlocks enchanting at its level and writing the next one, or it's a spare that makes writing cheaper.
  • Loot chests roll separately for each player, so nobody gets there first and empties them.
  • Librarians only sell comfort books.

Comfort versus power

Comfort

Cheap, early, no fighting

Efficiency, Silk Touch, Aqua Affinity, Respiration, Depth Strider, Feather Falling, Soul Speed, Swift Sneak, Frost Walker, Lure, Loyalty, Riptide.

Books only need tier 1 and 2 stuff.

Power and loot

Earned by going places

Sharpness, Protection, Power, Fortune, Looting and the rest of the combat enchantments.

Higher levels need Nether stuff. Top levels need far-away stuff, for the book and for each item.

Playing alone or together

Together

One big library

The group builds a library at the base and chips in at the table for the expensive books. Once a book is on the shelf, everyone enchants their own gear cheaply.

Alone

Your own library

Want to do your own thing far away? Build your own library and pay for every book yourself, or copy books at the group's library and take them home. Slower, but nobody can stop you. Friends can give you copies if they want to.

Nothing is tracked by the server. Your library helps someone else only if they use it or you give them books.

The good and the bad

Good

  • Enchanting from the first few sessions, not after diamonds
  • Builders get Efficiency V and Silk Touch without fighting
  • No random rolls: you always know what you need
  • Your progress is a place you can see and show off
  • Every found book is worth something, even duplicates
  • Anyone can chip in toward a book, whenever they pass by
  • Play together or alone, your choice
  • Shelving works like a chest, so there's nothing new to learn

Watch out for

  • Playing alone is much slower, on purpose. Is that fair enough?
  • Anyone can take books off a shared library's shelves
  • With no item limits, everyone eventually ends up with the same maxed gear. The climb is long, but it has an end.
  • Once a library is full, what's next? (The answer is later Seasons, but Season 1 might run dry.)
  • Copying could make one library seed everyone for almost nothing if it's too cheap
  • The very best enchantments cost a rare material on every item. Too harsh?
